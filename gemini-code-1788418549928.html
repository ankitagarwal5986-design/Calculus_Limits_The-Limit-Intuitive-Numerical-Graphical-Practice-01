The Limit: Foundations, Tables & Graphical Behavior

Review the intuitive concept of limits, one-sided limits, and reasons a limit may fail to exist before starting the quiz.

1. The Intuitive Definition of a Limit

Suppose \( f(x) \) is defined when \( x \) is near the number \( a \) (except possibly at \( a \) itself). We write: \[ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \] and say "the limit of \( f(x) \) as \( x \) approaches \( a \) equals \( L \)" if we can make the values of \( f(x) \) arbitrarily close to \( L \) by taking \( x \) to be sufficiently close to \( a \) from both sides, but not equal to \( a \).

2. Function Value vs. Limit Value

The limit only cares about what happens near \( x = a \), never what happens at \( x = a \). The actual function value \( f(a) \) can be completely different from \( \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \), or it can even be undefined.

3. One-Sided Limits & The Existence Criterion

  • Left-Hand Limit: \( \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = L_1 \) means \( x \) approaches \( a \) from values strictly less than \( a \).
  • Right-Hand Limit: \( \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L_2 \) means \( x \) approaches \( a \) from values strictly greater than \( a \).
  • Existence Theorem: The two-sided limit \( \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \) exists if and only if both one-sided limits exist and are equal to the same real number: \[ \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L \]

4. When Does a Limit Fail to Exist (DNE)?

  1. Jump Discontinuity: The left and right limits approach different finite numbers (\( \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) \neq \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) \)).
  2. Vertical Asymptote (Infinite Discontinuity): The function increases or decreases without bound (\( \pm\infty \)) near \( x = a \).
  3. Oscillating Behavior: The function values oscillate infinitely between bounds as \( x \to a \) (e.g. \( \sin(1/x) \)).
